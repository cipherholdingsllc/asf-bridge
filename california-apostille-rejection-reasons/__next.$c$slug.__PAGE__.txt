Understanding California apostille rejection reasons before submission is the single most effective way to avoid losing weeks to a resubmission cycle. <strong>Apostilles are issued by the California Secretary of State.</strong> Our role is to identify document and routing issues before submission — not after rejection.</p><p class="kicker glance-title">At a glance</p><dl class="glance"><div><dt>CA SOS Review</dt><dd>All submissions reviewed before apostille issues</dd></div><div><dt>Wrong Format</dt><dd>Photocopies and uncertified docs rejected</dd></div><div><dt>Expired Notary</dt><dd>Commission must be active at signing date</dd></div><div><dt>Wrong Route</dt><dd>Federal docs cannot use CA SOS path</dd></div><div><dt>Incomplete Packet</dt><dd>Missing fee, cover sheet, or return envelope</dd></div></dl><p><a href="/apostille-document-check/">Check My Document Before You Mail</a></p><p class="kicker">Predictable categories</p><h2>California Apostille Rejection Reasons — Overview</h2><p><strong>California apostille rejection reasons</strong> fall into predictable categories — wrong document format, expired notarization, incorrect routing, and incomplete packets. The California Secretary of State does not issue apostilles on documents that fail review, and rejected packets must restart the process entirely. Use <a href="/apostille-document-check/">Document Review</a> to check for issues before submission. Compare <a href="/california-apostille-guide/">The Guide</a> for process context, or review <a href="/apostille-vs-authentication-vs-legalization/">Apostille vs Authentication vs Legalization</a> if the route itself is uncertain.</p><p class="kicker">Why it matters</p><h2>Why California Apostille Rejection Matters</h2><p>A rejected apostille packet is not just an inconvenience — it restarts the clock entirely. The California Secretary of State returns rejected submissions without processing. The client must correct the defect, obtain replacement documents if necessary, and resubmit as a new request. Depending on the document type and correction required, this can add two to eight weeks or more to a time-sensitive international deadline.</p><p>Most <strong>California apostille rejection reasons</strong> are identifiable before submission. Document review catches the categories that produce the majority of rejections — wrong copy format, notarization defects, and routing errors. Starting with a review is not optional for high-stakes filings.</p><p class="kicker">Format</p><h2>Document Format Rejections</h2><p>The most common California apostille rejection reason is submitting the wrong copy type. The California Secretary of State requires either a certified copy issued by the official custodian of record, or an original document notarized by a California-commissioned notary public. Neither photocopies, hospital-issued informational copies, nor uncertified prints will receive an apostille regardless of how they are presented.</p><h3>Photocopy Rejection</h3><p>A plain photocopy of a birth certificate, marriage certificate, or any vital record is rejected. Only a certified copy issued by the county recorder or state registrar qualifies for the California apostille path.</p><h3>Informational Copy Rejection</h3><p>Some vital records offices issue “informational” copies that are not certified for legal use. These are clearly marked as such and are rejected by the California Secretary of State — only certified copies qualify.</p><h3>Altered Document Rejection</h3><p>Any document showing signs of alteration, whiteout, correction, or physical damage will be rejected. The California SOS requires documents to be in original unaltered condition as issued or notarized.</p><p class="kicker">Notarization</p><h2>Notarization Defect Rejections</h2><p>Notarization errors are the second-largest category of <strong>California apostille rejection reasons</strong>. The California Secretary of State verifies notary commission status against the Secretary of State’s own notary database. Any mismatch, expired commission, or incomplete notarization wording triggers a rejection.</p><h3>Expired Commission</h3><p>The notary’s commission must have been active on the date the document was signed and notarized. If the commission expired before or on the signing date, the California Secretary of State will reject the apostille request.</p><h3>Missing Notary Elements</h3><p>California notarizations require a complete acknowledgment or jurat with the notary’s name, county, commission number, commission expiration date, signature, and official seal. Any missing element is grounds for rejection.</p><h3>Out-of-State Notary</h3><p>The California Secretary of State only issues apostilles for documents notarized by a California-commissioned notary public. A document notarized in another state cannot receive a California apostille — it must be submitted to that state’s apostille authority.</p><p>Notary defects often require the document to be re-executed and re-notarized from scratch. Verifying commission status and notarization completeness before submission eliminates this risk.</p><p class="kicker">Routing</p><h2>Routing and Jurisdiction Rejections</h2><p>Routing errors send the right document to the wrong authority. These are entirely preventable and represent a significant category of <strong>California apostille rejection reasons</strong>. The California Secretary of State only has authority over California-issued public records and documents notarized by California notaries — any document outside that scope must go elsewhere.</p><h3>Federal Document Sent to CA SOS</h3><p>FBI Identity History Summaries, federal court records, and documents issued by federal agencies cannot receive a California apostille. These require OSCA federal authentication through the U.S. Department of State — mailing them to the California SOS results in return without processing.</p><h3>Non-Hague Destination</h3><p>If the destination country is not a Hague Convention member, a California apostille is not the correct certificate. The California SOS will still issue an apostille — but the destination country will not accept it, making the certificate useless and requiring the full authentication and legalization chain instead.</p><p>Confirm destination country Hague membership and document jurisdiction before any submission. See <a href="/apostille-vs-authentication-vs-legalization/">Apostille vs Authentication vs Legalization</a> for a full routing breakdown.</p><p class="kicker">Packet</p><h2>Packet and Submission Rejections</h2><p>Even a document that is otherwise eligible can be rejected if the submission packet is incomplete. The California Secretary of State returns packets missing required components — they do not process partial submissions or bill for missing fees separately.</p><h3>Missing or Incorrect Fee</h3><p>The California Secretary of State charges $20 per apostille request. Packets with the wrong fee amount, an expired check, or a missing payment are returned unprocessed. Confirm the current fee and accepted payment formats on the official SOS request page before mailing.</p><h3>Incomplete Cover Sheet</h3><p>The California SOS requires a completed cover sheet or request form with each submission. Omitting the cover sheet, leaving required fields blank, or using an outdated form version can result in rejection or processing delay.</p><h3>Missing Return Envelope</h3><p>Mail-in submissions must include a self-addressed return envelope with adequate postage or a prepaid trackable return label. Without a return envelope, the California SOS cannot return the apostilled document after processing.</p><p class="kicker">County clerk</p><h2>County Clerk Certification Rejections</h2><p>Many private documents — including powers of attorney, affidavits, and certain business documents — must pass through county clerk certification before reaching the California Secretary of State. If the county clerk step is skipped or done incorrectly, the California SOS will reject the apostille request at the state level.</p><h3>Missing County Clerk Step</h3><p>Privately notarized documents in most California counties must first be certified at the county clerk’s office where the notary is registered before submission to the California Secretary of State. Skipping this step is a direct rejection cause.</p><h3>Wrong County Clerk</h3><p>The county clerk certification must come from the county where the notary’s commission is on file. A certification from the wrong county does not satisfy the California SOS requirement and will result in rejection.</p><p class="kicker">After a rejection</p><h2>What to Do After a California Apostille Rejection</h2><p>If the California Secretary of State has already returned your packet, the first step is identifying the specific rejection reason from the return notice. The California SOS typically includes a notation with the returned documents indicating the defect category. Once identified, the correction path depends on the rejection type.</p><ol><li><strong>Read the return notice carefully.</strong> The California SOS notates rejection reasons on a standard form included with returned packets. Identify the specific defect before taking any action.</li><li><strong>Determine whether the document can be corrected or must be replaced.</strong> An expired notarization requires re-execution. A photocopy requires obtaining a new certified copy from the issuing authority. A routing error requires resubmitting to the correct authority entirely.</li><li><strong>Do not resubmit the same packet.</strong> Resubmitting an uncorrected or partially corrected packet wastes time and fees. The defect must be fully resolved before a new submission is prepared.</li><li><strong>Confirm all other checklist items</strong> before resubmitting — a rejection for one reason does not mean the rest of the packet is correct.</li><li><strong>Use document review</strong> on the corrected packet before resubmission to catch any remaining issues.</li></ol><p>If the rejection reason is unclear or the correction path is uncertain, start a <a href="/apostille-document-check/">document review</a> before proceeding.</p><h2>Official California Apostille Resources</h2><p>For current California apostille requirements, review the <a href="https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/request-apostille" rel="noopener">official California SOS apostille request page</a>, <a href="https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/processing-times" rel="noopener">current processing times</a>, and <a href="https://www.sos.ca.gov/notary/forms" rel="noopener">forms and fees</a>.
