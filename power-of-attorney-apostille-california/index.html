Pre-Check the POA

How a California Notary Public Acknowledgment Works on a POA

A Power of Attorney signed in California must be acknowledged before a California notary public to be apostille-eligible. The notary’s acknowledgment (Civil Code §1189) certifies that the principal personally appeared, was identified by satisfactory evidence, and acknowledged signing the document. The notary’s complete acknowledgment block includes: the notary’s signature, seal/stamp, printed name, commission number, commission expiration date, and county of commission.

The California Secretary of State then authenticates the notary’s signature by attaching the apostille. The apostille is a certificate that the notary’s signature is genuine and that the notary was commissioned at the time of the notarization — it does not authenticate the POA’s substance.

Substance Requirements Vary by Destination

The apostille step is uniform — but the underlying POA’s substance must satisfy the destination country’s law. Examples:

  1. Italy — often requires the POA to be drafted in Italian or accompanied by certified Italian translation; specific powers must be enumerated (general POAs are sometimes rejected); two witnesses may be required for real estate POAs in some regions.
  2. Mexico — POAs for real estate transactions usually require highly specific powers and may require ratification before a Mexican notary after the apostille is attached.
  3. Spain — requires the POA to be drafted before a U.S. notary in a form acceptable for use in the Spanish protocol notarial registry; specific notarial language requirements.
  4. U.K., Germany, France — generally accept a properly notarized and apostilled U.S. POA, but specific powers and translation may be required for the destination’s particular use.

Substance requirements change. Verify with the destination’s notarial authority, embassy/consulate, or the foreign attorney handling the transaction before notarizing.

Common Foreign Uses for a California POA Apostille

  1. International real estate — buying, selling, or managing property abroad while remaining in California.
  2. Foreign bank account management — granting authority to a representative to operate accounts.
  3. Inheritance proceedings — granting an executor or family member authority to act in a foreign probate.
  4. Business transactions abroad — signing contracts, registering subsidiaries, attending shareholder meetings.
  5. Healthcare and personal matters — granting medical or personal decision authority to a person abroad.

Common POA Apostille Rejections

  1. Notary commission expired as of the date of notarization. The most common rejection. Verify via the California Secretary of State notary lookup.
  2. Acknowledgment block incomplete — missing commission number, expiration, or county.
  3. Notary used a non-California seal (e.g., notary commissioned in Nevada notarized in California).
  4. Photocopy submitted instead of original notarized document.
  5. POA drafted in destination language without English translation — California notaries can only notarize in English (or for documents they can read); destination translation is typically done after the apostille is attached.
