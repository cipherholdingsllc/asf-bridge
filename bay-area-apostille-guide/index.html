Bay Area apostille guide · 9 counties

In seven of the nine Bay Area counties, a birth or death certificate can come from either the Clerk-Recorder or county Health. The Secretary of State apostilles a Clerk-Recorder copy directly. A Health copy carries a health officer’s signature and may need county clerk certification first. The table shows which office issues what.

The rule behind the table

Why the issuing office matters.

The California Secretary of State apostilles birth and death certificates signed by county clerks, county recorders and the State Registrar. When a certificate is signed by a health officer or local registrar, its guidance says county clerk certification “may be required,” or a certified copy from the county recorder or the state instead.

Since January 1, 2023, state law has required county health officers to file their oath with the Secretary of State (AB 2324). Its stated aim was to cut these rejections, but the Secretary of State’s guidance still lists the clerk step, and San Mateo, Marin, Santa Clara and Contra Costa still list it.

For records only Health holds so far, such as a very recent birth in Napa or Marin, or one under 3 years old in San Francisco, the Recorder route is not open yet. That is where the clerk step decides your timeline.
