No. A notarization is a California notary verifying your signature on a document. An apostille is a certificate issued by the California Secretary of State (or the U.S. Department of State for federal documents) that authenticates the official who signed or certified your document, so it is accepted in countries that belong to the Hague Apostille Convention. Some documents need notarization first, such as powers of attorney, affidavits and most corporate papers; certified vital records and court records go straight to the apostille step. When yours needs both, the $100 notarization add-on applies to the same-day and three-document tiers; it does not apply to the mail-in tier because most mail-in clients send documents already notarized.

It depends on who issued it. Documents issued or notarized in California (birth, marriage and death certificates, court records, diplomas, notarized and corporate documents) are apostilled by the California Secretary of State in Sacramento. Federal documents (an FBI Identity History Summary, USPTO or IRS records, federal court records) are apostilled by the U.S. Department of State in Washington, D.C. Sending a document to the wrong office is a rejection, which is why routing is the first thing we confirm.
